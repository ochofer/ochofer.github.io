Carlo Hofer is a quantitative researcher. His investment research takes questions that portfolio managers meet in practice and works them through on public and licensed data, with the code published. He holds a PhD in Politics and International Studies from the University of Warwick (2026) and is a Postdoctoral Research Associate at Queen Mary University of London, working with Prof. Maria Grasso on the Horizon Europe project [DEMETRA](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/demetra/){: target="_blank"}.

<p class="pcard__kicker">Signal evaluation</p>
<p class="pcard__title"><a href="/projects/volatility-managed-portfolios/">Volatility-managed portfolios: a replication, the real-time critique, and the months after the sample</a></p>
<p class="pcard__text">Volatility estimation and position sizing on Ken French's equity factors, replicating Moreira and Muir (2017) and the real-time critique of Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan (2020). Scaled by the inverse of last month's realised variance, a factor's Sharpe ratio is higher than the original's by a margin that is statistically significant at the 5 per cent level for momentum alone when each of the six factors is tested on its own, and for no factor after Holm's correction for the six tests. In the 136 months after Moreira and Muir's sample no factor's difference is statistically significant, and the intervals are too wide to rule out the differences found on their sample.</p>

<p class="pcard__kicker">Stock-level index tracking</p>
<p class="pcard__title"><a href="/projects/stock-level-tracking/">A point-in-time benchmark, a long-only mandate, and its performance attribution by sector</a></p>
<p class="pcard__text">A point-in-time S&amp;P 500 universe from CRSP and Compustat, a long-only mandate held close to its cap-weighted benchmark, and an attribution of its active return by sector. Under the mandate, simulated from 1999 to 2024, four covariance estimators realise 78.9 to 83.6 basis points a year of tracking error, and each forecast understates it: the realised active return is 48 to 85 per cent more volatile than forecast. Index changes force 11.4 per cent of the turnover on the Ledoit-Wolf estimator, and no estimator's mean active return is statistically significant at the 5 per cent level, which over these 306 months would take one of about 35 basis points a year.</p>

## Academic research

His academic research examines how the structures that surround people, online information environments on one hand and residential neighbourhoods on the other, condition political attitudes during periods of democratic stress. He works with causal-inference designs applied to panel data and survey experiments, complemented by geospatial analysis at fine spatial scales.

Carlo's PhD, supervised by [Prof. Vincenzo Bove](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/pais/people/bove){: target="_blank"} and [Dr Andreas Murr](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/pais/people/murr){: target="_blank"}, examined how online media environments and residential neighbourhoods shape political attitudes in the UK. Drawing on eleven waves of the British Election Study Internet Panel (over 100,000 observations), it combines within-person fixed-effects models, an instrumental-variables design, cross-lagged panel models, and fine-grained measures of neighbourhood segregation. The viva was held on 31 March 2026, with [Prof. Peter Thisted Dinesen](https://politicalscience.ku.dk/staff/academic_staff/?pure=en/persons/309060){: target="_blank"} (University of Copenhagen) and [Prof. Özlem Atikcan](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/pais/people/atikcan/){: target="_blank"} (University of Warwick) as examiners, and the thesis passed with no corrections. Before the PhD, he took an MSc in Management at Ca' Foscari University of Venice and a BSc in Management & Technology (Computer Science) at the Technical University of Munich.

- **Political humour (ESRC).** Co-authored projects on humorous political communication, covering climate change attitudes, intergroup affective polarisation and intergenerational hostility. The paper on intergroup humour, [Polarising punchlines](/publication/polarising-punchlines), was published in *Political Studies* in 2026.
- **From the PhD.** Two papers in preparation: one on online political attention and partisan affect, and one on neighbourhood composition, segregation and attitudes to immigration.
- **DEMETRA (Horizon Europe).** A seven-country, eleven-institution consortium on deliberative policy-making for sustainable food systems, running from 2024 to 2027. He coordinates across the consortium and wrote its first two [policy briefs](/publications/), published in 2026.
